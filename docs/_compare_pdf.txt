## Scorecards, Results & Live League Table 
 
* A **scorecard must be completed for every match**. 
* Make sure **you and your opponent have the correct handicaps entered on the scorecard 
before the match starts**. 
* The scorecard must be **fully completed and signed by both players**. 
* **20+ breaks must be recorded in the relevant boxes on the scorecard** so we can monitor 
player performances throughout the league. 
* If you make a break that you want to count for the **Highest Break prize**, it **must be 
declared and recorded on the scorecard**. A break cannot be claimed for the Highest Break 
prize afterwards if it was not declared on the scorecard. 
* Results will be recorded from the completed scorecards and a **live league table will be 
maintained throughout the league**. 
 
### Why the Scorecard Is Important 
 
The scorecard gives the **Tournament Directors** a clear record of every match. It allows us to 
deal with any queries or disputes, confirm the **correct handicaps were used**, monitor player 
performances, keep track of Highest Break claims and ensure the **live league table is accurate 
and up to date**. 
 
An **incomplete or unsigned scorecard**, or a match played from the **wrong handicap**, may 
result in the match being **void**. 
 
We have always used a **common-sense approach**, but please be aware that the 
**Tournament Directors have the right to void a match where they feel it is necessary**. 
 
If you are **in any doubt about anything**, please ask **Clive or Andy**. If we are not in the 
club, **give us a call**. It is always better to ask before playing the match than to have an issue 
afterwards.

For any players who are new to the league, **please don't be put off or intimidated by the rules 
below**. They are not there to make the league complicated or to take away from the enjoyment

The vast majority of matches will simply be played, the scorecard completed and everyone will 
get on with enjoying their snooker. These rules are there for the occasions when something

**The aim is simple: fair competition, good sportsmanship and an enjoyable league for 
everyone.** 
 
🚨 **MATCH RULES — PLEASE READ** 🚨 
 
## **1️⃣ WORLD SNOOKER RULES APPLY** 
 
**The official World Snooker / WPBSA Rules of Snooker apply to every match played in the 
league.** 
 
However, because our league is played in a club environment and matches are self-refereed, 
there are **3 specific CrossGuns League amendments** that will apply to help us manage the 
league fairly, consistently and smoothly. 
 
These are **league-specific amendments** to help us manage our competition. They do not 
replace the World Snooker rules generally — **World Snooker rules remain the foundation for 
every match.** 
 
The three league amendments are: 
 
**1️⃣ Club Miss Rule** 
**2️⃣ Pace of Play** 
**3️⃣ Player Conduct** 
 
--- 
 
# **2️⃣ CLUB MISS RULE** 
 
### **Club Rule — must be followed in all league matches** 

 
A miss may be called up to a maximum of **two (2) times**. 
 
This means a maximum of **3 fouls** can occur, with the **third and final foul unable to be 
called a miss**. 
 
After the third foul, play must proceed. 
 
The incoming player then has the option to: 
 
**A)** Play from the position in which the cue ball has come to rest; **or** 
 
**B)** Ask the opponent to play again from that position. 
 
In the event of a dispute regarding the application of the Club Miss Rule, the **league 
organiser's decision is final**. 
 
Players are expected to apply this rule fairly and in the spirit of the game. 
 
--- 
 
# **3️⃣ PACE OF PLAY** 
 
The official **World Snooker / WPBSA Rules of Snooker** cover time wasting under **Section 4 
– The Players, Rule 3: Time Wasting**. 
 
### **World Snooker – Section 4, Rule 3(a): Time Wasting** 
 
> *“In the event of a player taking an abnormal amount of time over a stroke, or the selection of 
a stroke, the referee shall warn the player that in the event of any further Time Wasting during 
the game, the frame will be awarded to their opponent.”* 
 
As our matches are self-refereed, the specific referee procedure cannot be applied in exactly 
the same way. However, the **principle behind the World Snooker rule will apply** to our league. 
 
Players are expected to maintain a **reasonable pace of play** throughout their matches. 
 
Taking additional time over difficult or important shots is perfectly acceptable. However, 
consistently slow play that unnecessarily prolongs a match is not. 
 
As a guide, a **best-of-three match taking in excess of three hours will normally be considered 
unusually long**. 
 

This is **not an automatic penalty**, as every match is different and tactical frames can naturally 
take longer. However, matches of this duration may be reviewed where concerns have been 
raised. 
 
### **Monitoring of Matches** 
 
While **league organisers and club staff are in the club**, they may observe league matches as 
part of their normal attendance. 
 
These observations, together with any concerns or reports received, may be taken into account 
when reviewing the pace of play. 
 
Where, following review, the league organisers determine that **excessive slow play has 
occurred**, a **2-point league deduction may be applied**. 
 
--- 
 
# **4️⃣ PLAYER CONDUCT** 
 
The official **World Snooker / WPBSA Rules regarding player conduct apply** to all league 
matches. 
 
Players are expected to conduct themselves in a **respectful and sporting manner** at all times. 
 
Friendly banter is part of club snooker, but persistent comments regarding an opponent's **luck, 
ability, shot selection**, or remarks intended to **distract, provoke or undermine** an opponent 
are not acceptable. 
 
There is a difference between normal good-natured banter and repeated behaviour that affects 
an opponent or interferes with the match. 
 
Players are expected to respect their opponent and allow them to play without unnecessary 
distraction or provocation. 
 
Where, following review, the league organisers determine that **persistent or repeated 
unsporting conduct has occurred**, a **2-point league deduction may be applied**. 
 
In more serious cases, or where such behaviour continues despite previous warnings or 
sanctions, the player may be **disqualified from the league**. 
 
In the event of disqualification, **the player's entry fee will not be refunded**. 
 
These measures are intended to protect the integrity of the league and ensure fairness for all 
participants.

🏆 **HIGHEST BREAK PRIZE — IMPORTANT** 🏆 
 
For the **Highest Break Prize**, a player's handicap will be added to their break **once a break 
of at least 26 has been achieved**. 
 
The minimum break of **26** is required before the handicap can be added. This ensures that a 
minimum of **6 balls have been legally potted** for a break to qualify. 
 
Once the 26-point minimum has been reached, the player's handicap is then added to their 
break total for the purposes of the Highest Break competition. 
 
The reason for applying the handicap is to give **every player, regardless of their playing level, 
a genuine opportunity to compete for the Highest Break Prize**. 
 
For example, a player making a **30 break with a 10-point handicap would have a qualifying 
Highest Break score of 40**. 
 
This system is designed to reward good break-building while also giving players of different 
abilities a fair chance to compete. 
 

**Remember: the break must be declared and recorded on the scorecard at the time it is made 
to qualify for the Highest Break Prize.** 🎱🏆

Groups C and D have **7 players**, so there has to be a bye each round. Because of this, some 
players may have played **4 matches** by the handicap review, while others may have played 
**3**. 
 
This is simply because there are **7 players instead of 8** — everyone will still have played at 
least 3 matches before the review. 
 
The advantage is that there are **4 qualifying places from 7 players**, giving everyone a **57% 
chance of qualifying**, compared with **50% in an 8-player group**. 
 
So, while some players may have **an extra match before the handicap review**, they also 
have a **better chance of qualifying** from a 7-player group.
