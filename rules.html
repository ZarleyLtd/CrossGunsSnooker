Scorecards, Results & Live League Table
  • A scorecard must be completed for every match.
  • Make sure you and your opponent have the correct handicaps entered on the scorecard before the match starts.
  • The scorecard must be fully completed and signed by both players.
  • 20+ breaks must be recorded in the relevant boxes on the scorecard so we can monitor player performances throughout the league.
  • If you make a break that you want to count for the Highest Break prize, it must be declared and recorded on the scorecard. A break cannot be claimed for the Highest Break prize afterwards if it was not declared on the scorecard.
  • Results will be recorded from the completed scorecards and a live league table will be maintained throughout the league.

Why the Scorecard Is Important

The scorecard gives the Tournament Directors a clear record of every match. It allows us to deal with any queries or disputes, confirm the correct handicaps were used, monitor player performances, keep track of Highest Break claims and ensure the live league table is accurate and up to date.

An incomplete or unsigned scorecard, or a match played from the wrong handicap, may result in the match being void.

We have always used a common-sense approach, but please be aware that the Tournament Directors have the right to void a match where they feel it is necessary.

If you are in any doubt about anything, please ask Clive or Andy. If we are not in the club, give us a call. It is always better to ask before playing the match than to have an issue afterwards.

A Note to All Players — Especially New Players

For any players who are new to the league, please don't be put off or intimidated by the rules below. They are not there to make the league complicated or to take away from the enjoyment of playing snooker. They are there to help the league run as smoothly, fairly and consistently as possible.

As the league has evolved, and with it now feeding into the CrossGuns Player of the Year Award, the importance of having clear procedures has increased. With 30 players competing for a €1,500 prize pot, it is essential that we have clear rules and measures in place so that everyone knows what is expected and, more importantly, that every player is treated fairly.

The vast majority of matches will simply be played, the scorecard completed and everyone will get on with enjoying their snooker. These rules are there for the occasions when something does go wrong, so that there is a clear and fair way of dealing with it.

The aim is simple: fair competition, good sportsmanship and an enjoyable league for everyone.

Match Rules — World Snooker Rules Apply

MATCH RULES — PLEASE READ

The official World Snooker / WPBSA Rules of Snooker apply to every match played in the league.

However, because our league is played in a club environment and matches are self-refereed, there are 3 specific CrossGuns League amendments that will apply to help us manage the league fairly, consistently and smoothly.

These are league-specific amendments to help us manage our competition. They do not replace the World Snooker rules generally — World Snooker rules remain the foundation for every match.

The three league amendments are:

  • 1. Club Miss Rule
  • 2. Pace of Play
  • 3. Player Conduct
Club Miss Rule

Club Rule — must be followed in all league matches

A miss may be called up to a maximum of two (2) times.

This means a maximum of 3 fouls can occur, with the third and final foul unable to be called a miss.

After the third foul, play must proceed.

The incoming player then has the option to:

A) Play from the position in which the cue ball has come to rest; or

B) Ask the opponent to play again from that position.

In the event of a dispute regarding the application of the Club Miss Rule, the league organiser's decision is final.

Players are expected to apply this rule fairly and in the spirit of the game.

Pace of Play

The official World Snooker / WPBSA Rules of Snooker cover time wasting under Section 4 – The Players, Rule 3: Time Wasting.

World Snooker – Section 4, Rule 3(a): Time Wasting

“In the event of a player taking an abnormal amount of time over a stroke, or the selection of a stroke, the referee shall warn the player that in the event of any further Time Wasting during the game, the frame will be awarded to their opponent.”

As our matches are self-refereed, the specific referee procedure cannot be applied in exactly the same way. However, the principle behind the World Snooker rule will apply to our league.

Players are expected to maintain a reasonable pace of play throughout their matches.

Taking additional time over difficult or important shots is perfectly acceptable. However, consistently slow play that unnecessarily prolongs a match is not.

As a guide, a best-of-three match taking in excess of three hours will normally be considered unusually long.

This is not an automatic penalty, as every match is different and tactical frames can naturally take longer. However, matches of this duration may be reviewed where concerns have been raised.

Monitoring of Matches

While league organisers and club staff are in the club, they may observe league matches as part of their normal attendance.

These observations, together with any concerns or reports received, may be taken into account when reviewing the pace of play.

Where, following review, the league organisers determine that excessive slow play has occurred, a 2-point league deduction may be applied.

Player Conduct

The official World Snooker / WPBSA Rules regarding player conduct apply to all league matches.

Players are expected to conduct themselves in a respectful and sporting manner at all times.

Friendly banter is part of club snooker, but persistent comments regarding an opponent's luck, ability, shot selection, or remarks intended to distract, provoke or undermine an opponent are not acceptable.

There is a difference between normal good-natured banter and repeated behaviour that affects an opponent or interferes with the match.

Players are expected to respect their opponent and allow them to play without unnecessary distraction or provocation.

Where, following review, the league organisers determine that persistent or repeated unsporting conduct has occurred, a 2-point league deduction may be applied.

In more serious cases, or where such behaviour continues despite previous warnings or sanctions, the player may be disqualified from the league.

In the event of disqualification, the player's entry fee will not be refunded.

These measures are intended to protect the integrity of the league and ensure fairness for all participants.

Important — Self-Refereed Matches

As our league matches are self-refereed, please do not get into arguments during a match regarding rules, pace of play or player conduct.

If you have a concern, report it to the league organisers / Tournament Directors afterwards, and the matter will be reviewed fairly and consistently.

If you are in any doubt about anything, please ask Clive or Andy.

If we are not in the club, give us a call.

It is always better to ask before playing the match than to have an issue afterwards.

The Bottom Line

The World Snooker / WPBSA Rules apply to every league match, with the following three specific CrossGuns League amendments:

1. CLUB MISS RULE
Our agreed Club Miss procedure.

2. PACE OF PLAY
Ensuring matches are played at a reasonable pace, with measures available where excessive slow play is determined to have occurred.

3. PLAYER CONDUCT
Ensuring everyone conducts themselves respectfully and in the spirit of the game, with appropriate sanctions available for persistent or serious misconduct.

These measures are not aimed at any individual player.

They are being introduced because the league has grown, now feeds into the CrossGuns Player of the Year Award, and with 30 players competing for €1,500, we need clear and consistent procedures in place.

Highest Break Prize

For the Highest Break Prize, a player's handicap will be added to their break once a break of at least 26 has been achieved.

The minimum break of 26 is required before the handicap can be added. This ensures that a minimum of 6 balls have been legally potted for a break to qualify.

Once the 26-point minimum has been reached, the player's handicap is then added to their break total for the purposes of the Highest Break competition.

The reason for applying the handicap is to give every player, regardless of their playing level, a genuine opportunity to compete for the Highest Break Prize.

For example, a player making a 30 break with a 10-point handicap would have a qualifying Highest Break score of 40.

This system is designed to reward good break-building while also giving players of different abilities a fair chance to compete.

Remember: the break must be declared and recorded on the scorecard at the time it is made to qualify for the Highest Break Prize.

Snooker Tournament

SNOOKER TOURNAMENT

Entry Fee: €50
Players: 31

Tournament Format

Group Stage

  • 4 groups in total.
  • 3 groups of 8 players.
  • 1 group of 7 players.
  • Players will be drawn into their groups, so there is an element of luck of the draw as to whether you are placed in a group of 7 or 8.
  • Players in a group of 8 will play 7 matches.
  • Players in a group of 7 will play 6 matches.
  • You play everyone in your group once.
  • The top 4 from each group qualify for the knockout stage.

Last 16

  • The top 4 from each group qualify.
  • That’s 16 players in total.
  • It is called the Last 16 because 16 players remain in the tournament.
  • Only 8 players play at this stage.

1st Place in Each Group

  • Go straight to the Quarter-Finals.

2nd Place in Each Group

  • Go straight to the Last 12.

3rd & 4th Place Finishers

  • The 3rd and 4th place finishers from all four groups go into a random draw.
  • They play 4 playoff matches.
  • The 4 playoff winners go through to the Last 12.
  • The 4 playoff losers are out but still receive prize money.

Last 12

The Last 12 is made up of:

  • The 4 group runners-up (2nd place).
  • The 4 playoff winners.
  • The 4 group winners are already waiting in the Quarter-Finals.

Last 12 Draw

  • The 4 group runners-up are seeded.
  • The 4 seeded runners-up cannot play each other.
  • Each group runner-up will play one of the 4 playoff winners.
  • The draw will decide which playoff winner each runner-up plays.
  • The 4 winners go through to the Quarter-Finals.

Quarter-Finals

  • The 4 group winners are seeded.
  • Each group winner will play one of the Last 12 winners.
  • Group winners cannot play each other.
  • The 4 winners go through to the Semi-Finals.

Semi-Finals

  • The 4 Quarter-Final winners play in the Semi-Finals.
  • The 2 winners go through to the Final.

Final

  • The 2 Semi-Final winners play for the tournament title.

Prize Money

  • 🏆 Winner – €525
  • 🥈 Runner-up – €250
  • 🥉 Semi-finalists – €150 each
  • Quarter-finalists – €50 each
  • Last 12 losers – €30 each
  • Playoff losers – €20 each

⭐ Highest Break – €75

Total Prize Money: €1,550

Every player who finishes in the top 4 of their group receives prize money.

Heads Up — Groups C & D

Groups C and D have 7 players, so there has to be a bye each round. Because of this, some players may have played 4 matches by the handicap review, while others may have played 3.

This is simply because there are 7 players instead of 8 — everyone will still have played at least 3 matches before the review.

The advantage is that there are 4 qualifying places from 7 players, giving everyone a 57% chance of qualifying, compared with 50% in an 8-player group.

So, while some players may have an extra match before the handicap review, they also have a better chance of qualifying from a 7-player group.
